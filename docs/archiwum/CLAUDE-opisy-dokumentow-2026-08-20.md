**`docs/swiat.md` is the world bible** — story, cast, village/forest layout, interiors and all narrative text, including a status table of which parts are already wired in. **`docs/wioska_plan.md` is the concrete village build plan** — a 30-resident roster (names, ages, households, sprite/interaction status), the measured layout, and the village economy, all read off the actual `.tmj` rather than off intent. Where the two disagree on village specifics (geography, headcounts, who lives where), `wioska_plan.md` wins — it records six decisions (D-1..D-6, its §10) that formally superseded `swiat.md`'s sketch, including the north-vs-south geography reversal noted below. `docs/prompt_wioska.md` is the meta-prompt that produced it, not content on its own — instructions for extending `wioska_plan.md`'s §5, never a source of game facts. `docs/ART_DIRECTION.md` is the visual style bible (measured cross-sheet palette overlap, pixel grid, per-sheet conventions) for every asset — creatures, buildings and NPCs alike. `docs/prompty_gemini.md` / `docs/prompt_sciany_gemini.md` log the Gemini image-generation prompts that worked (and why) for NPC portraits and wall tiles, so the next one doesn't start from a blank prompt. `docs/ZADANIE_drzewa.md` is a standing task brief for tree species art (5 of 9 species done). **`docs/warianty_atakow.md` + `docs/typy_obrazen.md` are the two halves of the combat system's newest layer** (weapon → variant → damage type → armour → quality → final damage), both written as analyses BEFORE the implementation and carrying the measurements that decided it. Read the first before touching `ITEMS[*].attacks`, the second before touching `ARMOR_PROFILE`, `EnemyConfig.armor` or `core/Protection.ts`. They exist mainly to hold the *reasons* — why reduction is percentage and not flat, why protection is counted in points and not percentages, why `slash` is deliberately the weakest against armour, why the spider is unarmoured — because every one of those will eventually be proposed again by somebody reading only the numbers. **`docs/pajaki_ocena.md` + `docs/pajak_jadowity_koncepcje.md` are the same pair of halves for the spiders** — the first is the self-contained briefing packet written for an outside reviewer (full sieciarz spec, the roster's occupied niches, the rules a new creature must not break); the second is the design battle that came out of it: three rival concepts for a melee spider, the six technical findings that killed the obvious versions of each, and — the part actually worth keeping — **the measured fight timeline that proved the first set of numbers made the venom's whole stacking curve invisible**. Read the second before touching `VENOM_SPIDER`, and read its `## Pomiar` section before touching any creature's HP, because the method (simulate weapon × attribute × play-style before writing the class) is the transferable part. ⚠ `warianty_atakow.md`'s header records the **two** decisions the implementation reversed afterwards (the sword thrust's `swingMul`, and the second variant going to the SIEKIERA rather than the toporek), and why. **`docs/ekwipunek_mechanika.md` + `docs/ekwipunek_przedmioty.md` are the entry point for anything worn or wielded**, and they split on a rule worth keeping: the first is hand-written and holds **how it works and what a new item may not do** (every field on an item, both damage pipelines end to end, 15 numbered invariants, per-kind checklists for adding a weapon/armour piece/off-hand); the second is **generated** by `tools/katalog_ekwipunku.mjs` straight out of `GameConfig.ts` (esbuild → node, real values, not text parsing) and holds the whole roster with derived DPS/root/mitigation plus the slot×material and distance×damage-type **gap grids** that say where a new item would not duplicate anything. Read `ekwipunek_mechanika.md` before adding any equippable; never hand-edit a number in `ekwipunek_przedmioty.md` — change `GameConfig.ts` and re-run the generator, which is a przymiar (report only, writes solely with `--zapisz`) and self-checks its mirrored formulas against the 53,3%/35,1% figures this file cites. **`docs/bron_dwureczna_analiza.md` is the two-handed-weapon foundation — analysed, reviewed (GO) and ✅ IMPLEMENTED 2026-08-16**, with **`docs/wlocznia_analiza.md`** the measurement pass for its first and only user, `ITEMS.spear` (Włócznia — reach 98, arc 40°, `pierce`, one variant, Siła 3, 150g, on the Kowal's shelf). ⚠ **Read the spear doc's correction block before touching ANY weapon's reach**, because it holds the session's biggest discovery and it is not about the spear: `GameScene.targetInReach` compares `distance − allowance ≤ reach`, where `allowance` is 35% of the target's display size — so the threshold for reaching a wolf's 96px orbit is **reach ≥ 78.36, not 96**, and hittability jumps from **22.2% to 97.1% of the wolf's cycle between reach 78 and 79**. The hammer sits **0.36 px** below that cliff (and 1.3 px below the sieciarz's band), i.e. the game's longest weapon is on a knife-edge against two enemies at once — the same `blockArcDeg` class of defect, already shipped, now parked in `odlozone.md`. Reach is therefore a **binary switch, not a dial**, for every weapon in this game; the spear's 98 was chosen to clear it by ~20px rather than by luck. Verified in-engine (not just `tsc`) by temporarily marking the hammer 2H and driving `window.dev` — a full bag refuses with the exact missing count, freeing one slot lets it through (confirming the `F ≥ 1` arithmetic), an illegal weapon+shield pair restored from a save is normalised away, and the same pair with a full bag is **tolerated with nothing lost**; the temporary marking was reverted after the measurements. The pieces: `itemHands()` (Items.ts), `Inventory.freeSlotCount`, `Equipment.usesBothHands`/`handsRefusalFor`/`normalizeHands`/`describeHandsRefusal`, the `Save.load()` normalisation call, and the doll veil + refusal messages in PlayerPanel/ItemTooltip. Read its `⚑ REVIEW ZAMKNIĘTY` header before the body: nine numbered corrections there supersede the analysis underneath, and two body sections (D.6, F.2) are marked 🚫 because acting on them would now be wrong. The approved model is one optional field — `hands?: 1 | 2`, absent meaning 1 — and nothing else: no damage/speed/range multiplier, no block penalty, no Strength scaling. Four things are worth carrying in your head. ⚠ **`hands` describes a concrete ITEM, never a weapon family** — a 1H hammer and a 2H hammer may coexist, which is why "reclassify the axe and hammer" was withdrawn. ⚠ **The block needs ZERO special cases and that is the model's correctness test**: `blockSpec` reads the offhand slot, so an empty offhand already yields block 0 / swingMul 1 / 0 armour points — if an implementation ever needs `if (twoHanded)` in there, the foundation is wrong. ⚠ **`hands: 2` alone is NOT sufficient, because `Save.ts` (lines 365–376) writes straight into `Equipment.weapon`/`.offhand` and bypasses `equip()` entirely** — verified by grep to be the only such place in `src/`, so there is exactly one boundary to normalise, and the rule is "an action can be refused, an already-existing state cannot" (a full backpack on load tolerates the illegal pair rather than destroying an item). ⚠ **Validation has TWO directions and `accepts()` covers only one** — an offhand item onto a 2H character is refused for free, but a 2H weapon onto an occupied offhand slips through, because `slotForItem` always answers `"weapon"`. Separately, the doc records a measured balance case that is **not** a global rule: młot + tarcza okuta roots for 328 ms against the skoczek's 480 ms tell, i.e. **−15 ms — the designed counterplay is already broken in shipped code, by a ONE-handed weapon with a shield.** Do not turn that into a `MAX_ROOT_MS`. ⚠ **That instruction stands, but the reason has changed and the −15 ms figure itself is now SUPERSEDED — see `docs/macierz_walki.md` before quoting either.** **`docs/macierz_walki.md` + `tools/macierz_walki.mjs` are the sweep of the whole matrix** (2026-08-17): all 120 `weapon × offhand × telegraph` triples and every weapon's reach against the two enemies that hold a distance, generated out of `GameConfig.ts` the same esbuild→node way `katalog_ekwipunku.mjs` is, and a przymiar in the same sense (writes only with `--zapisz`). Three results worth carrying: **(1)** a global `MAX_ROOT_MS` should NOT exist — three of the four telegraphs fit every root in the game at Zręczność 0 with any shield, and each telegraph has its own designed counter (only two of the four are answered with footwork at all). **(2)** the −15 ms measurement **does not reproduce; it comes out +72 ms**, because the escape is 80 ms and not ~167: `VenomSpider.enterWindup` calls `setVelocity(0,0)` so the creature stands still through its whole tell, and the gap to cross is `bite.reach − bite.range` = 12 px. ⚠ **Consequence for any future measurement here: the distance to escape does NOT depend on the player's weapon** — the enemy closes to *its* own range, so a hammer user runs exactly as far as a dagger user; only the root differs. **(3)** the hammer's 0.36 px is not an outlier — **every weapon except the spear is outside the wolf's orbit** (stick by 4.36 px, sword by 10.36, dagger by 22.36), so moving the hammer to 79 would not fix a rounding error, it would make the hammer the only non-spear weapon you can stand still and hit a circling wolf with. The one genuinely tight telegraph is the Szary (167 ms budget, under every weapon's root including bare fists) — and `GreyWolf.interrupt`'s own comment names the blunt interrupt, not footwork, as "the one counter", so that is design rather than defect. **`docs/referencje_zewnetrzne.md` is the registry for `public/assets/tem/`** — 15 external reference images (isometric roof/building/farm/prop packs, licence unresolved), each with a one-paragraph capsule, a style-fit verdict, and — where one exists — a named connection to a planned-but-unbuilt piece of content (the Saltbox roof shape → the still-blocked mono-pitch annex; the chicken sprites → the empty coop; the notice-board prop → `wioska_plan.md` §7.3 B10). **None of it is loaded by the game or safe to crop 1:1** — same "reference only, shape vocabulary only" rule this file already states for `b1.jpg`/`a1.jpg` at the top level; this doc is what stops the next reference drop from sitting undocumented the way these 15 files did.
